Context

Project overview

KMS Lighthouse is an enterprise knowledge platform behind the call centers and self-service portals of companies like DHL, GE Healthcare, and Allianz.

The Help Center is its customer-facing product — a fully branded self-service site that pulls content straight from the platform teams already use: no separate site, no migration, no parallel workflow. It ships as two connected surfaces: an admin panel where business owners configure and brand the site, and the end-user experience where readers find answers.

context

My role

Sole designer across research, IA, flows, UI, and design-system work, partnering with PMs and engineers over multiple phases.

The core challenge: serve two audiences in one product — the admin configuring the site, and the end user trying to find an answer fast — without letting either one's complexity leak into the other.

Research

Problems

  • No admin control. Every change — copy, layout, branding — went through a specialized dev team. Admins couldn't touch their own site.
  • Not built for mobile. Most users seek help on their phone; the legacy product wasn't responsive, answers were hard to find, and tickets went up.
  • Barely customizable. Enterprises couldn't make it feel like their brand — a dealbreaker for a product sold as a differentiator.

A differentiator that didn't differentiate. Clients passed; prospects went elsewhere.

research

Goals

  • Put admins in control — own setup, content, and branding without dev or support.
  • Design mobile-first — around one user, one answer, fast.
  • Make it brandable — real control over look and layout, so it feels like the customer's own site.

research

Wireframes

Structure before style: density, navigation, and hierarchy resolved across both admin and end-user screens before any visual design entered.

Admin panel

Admin panel panels

Where the product earns its keep. If admins can't run the Help Center themselves, nothing else matters.

The panel is organized into focused areas — customization, content, localization, and settings — each a self-serve surface that replaces what used to be a dev ticket.

admin

Homepage widgets

Admins compose the homepage from modular widgets — hero search, category cards, featured content, news, and more — arranging and configuring each one to fit their audience.

admin

Editing the help center

Admins edit the site widget by widget against a live preview of the real page — changing copy, layout, and branding and seeing the result immediately, no code and no publish-and-pray.

admin

Settings

Website, content, localization, and translation settings — presented as a carousel of focused panels so admins move through configuration one clear step at a time.

End user experience

End-user experience

For end users, success is one thing: the right answer, fast. A hero search anchored in the brand's own identity, category cards mapped to the most common tasks, and clean article pages with related links and step-by-step guides.

An AI Answer feature streams a direct response above the results, closing with the sources it drew on and a plain caveat that it isn't infallible.

enduser

Mobile

Mobile-first as a constraint, not a slogan — the same search-first hierarchy holds on the smallest screen, where most users actually seek help.
